Camera exposure refers to the amount of light that reaches the camera's sensor when capturing an image, which affects the brightness and quality of the photograph. It is controlled by three main settings:
 
1. **Aperture** : The size of the lens opening, measured in f-stops (e.g., f/1.8, f/5.6). A wider aperture (lower f-stop) allows more light to enter, resulting in a brighter image but with a shallower depth of field.
 
2. **Shutter Speed** : The length of time the camera's shutter is open, measured in fractions of a second (e.g., 1/1000, 1/30). A faster shutter speed reduces the amount of light but freezes motion, while a slower speed lets in more light but may cause motion blur.
 
3. **ISO** : The camera sensor's sensitivity to light, with higher ISO values (e.g., ISO 1600) making the sensor more sensitive. Higher ISO can brighten the image in low-light conditions but may introduce noise.

Together, these settings are known as the "exposure triangle." Balancing them properly helps achieve the right exposure based on lighting conditions, desired depth of field, motion, and image clarity.
